Can we undo
the matrix?

A matrix moves the first basis vector and the second basis vector. The grid and every point in space follow. But can we always find our way back?

Watch the shaded square. Its original area is 1. After the transformation, it becomes a parallelogram whose area is the absolute value of the determinant.

Area = |det(M)|

Orientation:

The controls set the target matrix A. The scrubber displays M(t) = (1 − t)I + tA: identity at 0%, A at 100%. The readout always describes what you currently see.

Before transformation: P is cyan, Q is an orange ring. Their outputs MP and MQ appear on the main grid. This example pair is chosen automatically for the displayed matrix; it can change as you scrub.

Try it

Choose Collapse to line, then press play or scrub from left to right. Watch the area shrink to zero as the two outputs merge.

At 100%, change d from 0 to 0.1. A thin parallelogram has reappeared: nonzero area makes the matrix invertible again.

Now choose Reflect. A negative determinant means reversed orientation, not negative area. The final matrix is still invertible.

A reflection’s animation passes through a singular matrix halfway. That intermediate collapse does not make the final reflection singular.
